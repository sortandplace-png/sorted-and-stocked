# How To Track Everything In Your Home

You don't need to track everything. That's the mistake that kills every system in week three.

You need to track the things that cost you something when you don't know about them.

---

## Three categories, and only three

**Consumables.** What runs out — food, cleaning supplies, paper goods, toiletries. Not knowing costs you duplicate purchases, spoiled food, and emergency trips.

**Valuables.** What you'd need to list for insurance after a fire or a theft. Not knowing costs you a claim you can't substantiate.

**Maintenance.** What needs doing on a schedule — filters, gutters, servicing. Not knowing costs you a repair instead of a maintenance job.

Everything else in your house is just possessions. Tracking them earns you nothing.

---

## Each one is a different job

**Consumables need quantity and a low point.** Two rolls of foil left, not zero. Updated by whoever takes the last one, which means everyone needs access.

**Valuables need description, value and proof.** Photographs, serial numbers, receipts where you have them. Updated once a year, and **stored somewhere other than the house** — a record that burns with the house is no record.

**Maintenance needs a date and a repeat.** Not a list you check, a reminder that comes to you. A list you have to remember to look at has the same problem as remembering the task.

Trying to run all three in one tool is why most people end up with none.

---

## Start where it costs most

If you're re-buying things you already own — **consumables**.

If you've never listed what you own and you'd struggle after a fire — **valuables**.

If things keep breaking that could have been serviced — **maintenance**.

Pick one. Do one shelf, one room, or four reminders. Leave it a fortnight and see whether it stayed true.

---

## For a kosher kitchen

Every consumable needs its designation on it, and locations that describe a kitchen with two of everything.

Pesach and Yom Tov sets are worth tracking separately, because the question about them is "do we have enough" once a year rather than "are we low" weekly.

---

## Why systems fail

**Scope.** Someone starts with the whole house and stops at forty percent.

**Solo.** It lives on one person's phone, so it's that person's job forever and drifts out of date immediately.

**No return.** Recording without a low-stock signal or a reminder means you're maintaining a database for its own sake.

**Fifty items you keep accurate beats four hundred you catalogued once.**

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should I actually track in my home?

Three things: consumables that run out, valuables you'd need to list for insurance, and maintenance that runs on a schedule. Everything else is just possessions, and tracking them earns you nothing.

### Can one system track all of it?

Not well. Consumables need quantity and a low point updated by whoever takes the last one. Valuables need photographs and values updated yearly and stored outside the house. Maintenance needs a reminder that comes to you. Trying to run all three in one tool is why people end up with none.

### Where should I start?

Wherever it's costing you most — consumables if you're re-buying things you own, valuables if you'd struggle to list what you had after a fire, maintenance if things keep breaking that could have been serviced. One shelf, one room, or four reminders.

### Why do home tracking systems stop working?

Three reasons: the scope was the whole house so it stalled at forty percent, it lives on one person's phone so it stays their job and drifts, or there's no low-stock signal or reminder so it records without returning anything.
