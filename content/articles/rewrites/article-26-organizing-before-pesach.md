# Organizing A Home Before Pesach

**Before anything else:** this is not a halacha guide. What constitutes chametz, what requires cleaning versus checking, kashering, bedikas chametz, mechiras chametz, and every question of what is actually required — all of that goes to your Rav. Nothing here is a psak.

What follows is the organizing layer that sits alongside it.

---

## The distinction that saves the most

Pesach cleaning concerns chametz — not dust, not general grime, not the annual deep clean. A spotless windowsill is a different project.

Rabbanim raise this publicly every year, precisely because the conflation causes real strain in households.

**Ask your Rav where the line is for your home.** Then organize around the answer rather than around the anxiety.

Once you know what's actually required, the work splits into two streams that run separately:

**What Pesach requires** — defined by your Rav, non-negotiable, and usually far smaller than it feels in advance.

**What you've decided to do as well** — optional, and worth deciding on purpose rather than by accumulation.

---

## Start six weeks out, with what leaves

The single biggest reduction in pre-Pesach work is having less to deal with.

**Playroom.** Toys nobody plays with, puzzles missing pieces, anything outgrown. Toys that have been in mouths or in the car are frequently a chametz question rather than a clutter question — **ask your Rav** which categories need what.

**Closets.** Outgrown clothing, shoes nobody wears.

**Surfaces.** Bookshelves, side tables, the places things accumulate.

**The car.** The single most-forgotten space and a genuine chametz location — seats, car seats, between and under seats, the trunk, the door pockets.

---

## The kitchen

The kitchen carries the most halachically significant work of the season, and this stays entirely on the organizing side of it. **What gets kashered, what gets covered, what gets put away, and how — ask your Rav.**

What organizing can do:

**Decide before you start where the chametz kitchen goes.** Discovering that mid-transfer is the most common way the day runs long.

**Make it contained, labelled, and legible to someone else.** A guest or a helper should be able to tell what's what without asking you.

**Label in Spanish too** if that's what your staff reads.

---

## The mistakes that cost the most

**Treating cleaning, organizing and what's required as one project.** They're three, and only one of them is defined by halacha.

**No plan for where the chametz kitchen goes**, discovered mid-transfer.

**Starting with the kitchen.** It's the hardest and it's last for a reason.

**Not deciding what you're not doing.** The standards nobody chose are where the exhaustion comes from.

---

## Afterwards

Whatever you built to contain and label this year — keep it. Pesach comes round again, and the households that find it easier are the ones where last year's system is still intact and still labelled.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Where do I start organizing before Pesach?

Six weeks out, with what leaves rather than what gets cleaned — playroom, closets, surfaces, and the car, which is the most forgotten space and a genuine chametz location. Having less to deal with is the single biggest reduction in the work.

### Is Pesach cleaning the same as spring cleaning?

No. They're separate projects that happen to fall at the same time — Pesach cleaning concerns chametz, spring cleaning concerns dust and general upkeep. Conflating them is the most common cause of pre-Pesach exhaustion. Ask your Rav what is actually required for your home, then decide separately how much else you want to take on.

### What can organizing actually do about the kitchen?

Decide before you start where the chametz kitchen goes, and make whatever you set up contained, labelled and legible to someone else — a guest or helper should be able to tell what's what without asking. What gets kashered, covered or put away, and how, goes to your Rav.

### What's the most common pre-Pesach mistake?

Treating cleaning, organizing and what's halachically required as one project when they're three, and only one is defined by halacha. Close behind: having no plan for where the chametz kitchen goes, and never deciding what you're not going to do.
