# Meal Planning Systems For Busy Families

Meal planning fails for a boring reason: people plan meals they'd like to cook rather than meals they will cook on a Wednesday at six.

The plan isn't wrong. The week is.

---

## Plan the week you actually have

Look at the calendar first, not the recipes.

**Which nights are you home? Which are late? Who's out?**

A Tuesday with two pickups doesn't get a recipe with a marinade. It gets something that was already made, or something that takes fifteen minutes.

Match the effort to the evening and the plan survives.

---

## Fewer meals than you think

You don't need seven different dinners. Most families rotate about a dozen things and are perfectly happy.

**Write down the ones that already work.** That list is your plan — you're arranging it, not inventing it every Sunday.

New recipes belong on the nights you have room for them, not on a Tuesday.

---

## Cook once for more than once

Not full batch cooking, which is its own project. Just: **when you make something that reheats, make more of it.**

Soups, roasts, kugels, anything braised. Doubling costs almost no extra time and buys you a night.

---

## Build the list from the plan

The point of planning isn't the menu. It's that the shopping list writes itself, and you stop the emergency trip.

**Check what you have before you write it.** Most duplicate buying happens because nobody looked.

---

## For a kosher kitchen

**Fleishig, milchig and pareve across the week** — the plan has to hold that, and a generic meal planner won't.

**Shabbos is not a normal night.** It's the biggest cooking of the week and it needs its own planning, working backwards from candle lighting.

**Erev Shabbos gets its own column.** The day runs differently and scheduling it as a weekday is why it goes long.

**Yom Tov is a project**, not a busier week — start earlier than feels necessary.

---

## Twenty minutes on Sunday

Calendar. Meals against the nights. List built from what's missing.

That's the whole system. **Almost everything that goes wrong in a household week was visible on Sunday and nobody looked.**

---

## When it slips

Skip and carry on. Don't cook Tuesday's meal on Wednesday and push everything down a day — that's how one bad night becomes an abandoned plan.

The plan is a default, not a debt.

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why does meal planning keep failing?

Because people plan meals they'd like to cook rather than meals they'll actually cook on a Wednesday at six. Look at the calendar before the recipes and match the effort to the evening — a night with two pickups doesn't get a recipe with a marinade.

### How many meals do I actually need to plan?

Fewer than you think. Most families rotate about a dozen dinners and are perfectly happy. Write down the ones that already work — that list is your plan, and you're arranging it rather than inventing a new week every Sunday.

### How do I plan around Shabbos and Yom Tov?

Give Erev Shabbos its own column, because the day doesn't run like a weekday and scheduling it as one is why it runs long. Plan Shabbos backwards from candle lighting, and treat Yom Tov as a project rather than a busier week — start earlier than feels necessary.

### What do I do when the plan falls apart mid-week?

Skip and carry on. Don't push everything down a day, because that's how one bad night turns into an abandoned plan. The plan is a default, not a debt.
