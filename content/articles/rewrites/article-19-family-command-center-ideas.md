# Family Command Center Ideas

A command center is one wall that answers the questions people keep asking you.

What's happening today. Where's the permission slip. Who's picking up. When does the cleaner come.

If those questions still route through you, you don't have one yet — you have a nice-looking wall.

---

## Where it goes

Not where it looks best. **Where people already stop.**

Usually just inside the door people actually use, or the end of the kitchen where everyone passes. If it's in the hallway nobody walks down, it will be beautiful and ignored within a month.

The test: stand where your family drops their bags. That's the spot.

---

## What earns a place on it

**A calendar everyone can see.** Not your phone. Something visible from across the room, showing this week — not the month, which is too much information to scan while putting shoes on.

**A landing zone.** Hooks, a shelf, a basket. Keys, bags, the things that otherwise become the pile at the bottom of the stairs.

**Somewhere for paper to go.** Three slots is enough: needs doing, needs filing, take it with you. Paper that lands here doesn't land on the counter.

**A note surface.** Whiteboard, chalkboard, a pad. For the thing you'd otherwise text yourself.

That's it. Four things.

---

## What ruins them

**Too much on the wall.** Meal plan, chore chart, weather, inspirational quote, month view, week view, menu. Nobody reads a wall that dense — it becomes wallpaper within a fortnight.

**Hooks too high for the children.** If a child can't hang their own bag, you're hanging it. Put the hooks where their hands are, not where they look tidy.

**No paper destination.** The single most common failure. Without one, everything still ends up on the kitchen counter and the wall is decorative.

**One person maintains it.** Same failure as everything else in the house. If only you update the calendar, it's still your job — you've just moved it onto a wall.

---

## For a frum household

A few things worth building in rather than adding later.

**Candle lighting time** for this week, visible without anyone checking a phone.

**Erev Shabbos as its own column** — the day works differently from the rest of the week and a normal grid doesn't reflect that.

**Yom Tov ahead**, far enough out that the shopping and the cooking can start when they need to rather than the night before.

**Staff schedule** if you have help — who's in on which day, so nobody has to ask you.

---

## Start with a week

Don't build the whole thing.

Put up the calendar and the hooks. Use it for a week. See which questions people still ask you — those are the things that need to go on the wall next.

Most people build a command center around what they imagine their family needs, then find the family keeps asking about something else entirely. A week of watching costs nothing and tells you more than any plan.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should a family command center include?

Four things: a calendar visible from across the room showing this week, a landing zone for keys and bags, a defined destination for paper, and a note surface. More than that and it becomes wallpaper that nobody reads.

### Where should I put a family command center?

Where people already stop — usually just inside the door your family actually uses, or the end of the kitchen everyone passes through. Stand where they drop their bags; that's the spot. A command center in a hallway nobody walks down gets ignored within a month.

### Why do family command centers stop working?

Usually because there's too much on the wall to scan quickly, because the hooks are too high for children to use themselves, because there's no paper destination so everything still lands on the counter, or because only one person updates it — which means it's still that person's job.

### How do I set one up for Shabbos and Yom Tov?

Build in candle lighting time for the week, give Erev Shabbos its own column since the day runs differently from a normal weekday, show Yom Tov far enough ahead that shopping and cooking can start on time, and include the staff schedule if you have help so nobody has to ask.
