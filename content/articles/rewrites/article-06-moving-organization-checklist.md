# Moving Organization Checklist: The Complete Guide

Moving goes wrong in the same place every time: people start packing before they've decided what's coming.

Then they pay to move things they don't want, unpack them into a house that has no room, and spend the next six months getting rid of them.

So the checklist starts before the boxes.

---

## Eight weeks out

**Decide what isn't coming.** This is the whole job. Every box you don't pack is money you don't spend and a decision you don't make twice.

**Get quotes.** Three movers, in writing, including whether they pack.

**Start using things up** — freezer, pantry, cleaning supplies. Don't move half-full bottles.

---

## Six weeks out

**Book the movers.** Good ones go early, especially at the end of a month.

**Start with the rooms you don't live in.** Storage, garage, guest room, the back of closets. Nothing there is needed before the move.

**Order supplies.** More boxes than you think, and better tape than you think.

---

## Four weeks out

**Book the utilities** at both ends, with a day of overlap. Internet first — it has the longest lead time of anything.

**Change of address:** post, bank, insurance, employer, licence, pharmacy, subscriptions, and anything that only contacts you once a year.

**Start packing properly.** Books, off-season clothes, anything decorative.

---

## Two weeks out

**Pack everything except what you use daily.**

**Label by room and by priority**, not by contents. "Kitchen — first day" tells someone where to put it and when to open it. "Assorted" tells nobody anything.

**Photograph anything with cables** before you unplug it.

---

## The last week

**Pack the first-night box.** Bedding, towels, toiletries, chargers, medication, kettle, a change of clothes for everyone. This box travels with you, not on the truck.

**Keep documents and valuables with you.**

**Confirm arrival times** with the movers and with whoever's letting you in.

---

## For a frum household

**Check the eruv** before you commit, and confirm walking distance to shul rather than driving distance.

**Schools and bus routes** for the actual address.

**Kosher groceries and delivery ranges.**

**And the kitchen** — a move is the one real chance to design separation into the layout rather than working around one. Decide which cabinets, drawers, counters and sinks carry which designation before anything goes in, and label as you place.

Anything about status of what you're bringing, or the kitchen you're arriving at, goes to your Rav.

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When should I start organizing a move?

Eight weeks out, and start by deciding what isn't coming rather than by packing. Every box you don't pack is money you don't spend moving it and a decision you don't have to make again at the other end.

### How should I label moving boxes?

By room and by priority rather than by contents — 'Kitchen, first day' tells someone where to put it and when to open it. Contents labels tell nobody anything useful while they're carrying it through a door.

### What goes in a first-night box?

Bedding, towels, toiletries, chargers, medication, a kettle and a change of clothes for everyone. It travels with you rather than on the truck, along with documents and valuables.

### What should a frum family check before moving?

Eruv boundaries, walking distance to shul rather than driving distance, schools and the bus routes that serve the actual address, and kosher grocery delivery ranges. A move is also the one real chance to design kitchen separation into the layout — decide designations before anything goes into a cabinet.
