# Packing Tips For Moving Like A Professional Organizer

Most people pack a room the way they'd tidy it — a bit of everything into whatever box is open.

Then they arrive with forty boxes marked "misc" and no idea what's in any of them.

Professionals pack for the unpacking. That's the whole difference.

---

## Pack by room, unpack by room

Everything from one room goes in boxes marked for that room. Nothing mixed.

It sounds obvious and almost nobody does it, because at the end you're tired and the last box takes whatever is left.

**Keep an "odds" box per room** for exactly that, and label it as such. One per room, not one per house.

---

## Label for the person carrying it

Room first, then priority. **"Kitchen — first day."** "Bedroom — later."

Not contents. Nobody reads a list while holding a box, and by the time you want to know what's inside you're already standing in the right room.

**Write on the side, not the top.** Boxes get stacked.

---

## Heavy small, light big

Books in small boxes. Bedding in big ones.

A large box of books cannot be lifted, will break, and will hurt someone. This is the most common packing mistake and the only one that causes injuries.

---

## Fill every box completely

Half-full boxes collapse when stacked. Overfull boxes split.

Fill gaps with towels, linens, soft things you're moving anyway. That's free padding and it means fewer boxes overall.

---

## The things people forget

**Photograph the back of anything with cables** before unplugging. Television, router, desk setup.

**Keep screws and fittings with their furniture** — bag them and tape the bag to the piece itself, not into a box of hardware you'll never find.

**Don't pack liquids with anything absorbent.** Cleaning supplies and toiletries go together, upright, in a lined box.

**Wardrobe boxes for hanging clothes** are worth the money — clothes go straight from rail to rail and you skip an entire folding-and-unfolding cycle.

---

## The first-night box

Bedding, towels, toiletries, chargers, medication, kettle, basic tools, a change of clothes for everyone.

**It travels with you, not on the truck.** Label it so obviously that nobody can bury it.

---

## For a kosher kitchen

Pack the designations separately and label them clearly, so nothing gets unpacked into the wrong cabinet at the other end when everyone is tired.

Keep Pesach things sealed and boxed separately from the rest.

Anything about status of what you're packing, or how it should be handled, goes to your Rav.

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How should I pack boxes when moving?

By room, with nothing mixed between rooms, and label for the person carrying the box — room first, then priority, written on the side rather than the top. Contents labels don't help; by the time you want to know what's inside, you're already in the right room.

### What's the most common packing mistake?

Heavy things in large boxes. Books belong in small boxes and bedding in large ones — a big box of books can't be lifted safely, will split, and is the only packing error that regularly causes injuries.

### What should go in the first-night box?

Bedding, towels, toiletries, chargers, medication, a kettle, basic tools and a change of clothes for everyone. It travels with you rather than on the truck, labelled obviously enough that nobody can bury it.

### What do people always forget when packing?

Photographing the back of anything with cables before unplugging it, taping screws and fittings to the furniture they belong to rather than into a hardware box, and keeping liquids upright and away from anything absorbent.
