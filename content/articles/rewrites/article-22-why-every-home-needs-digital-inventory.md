# Why Every Home Needs A Digital Inventory System

You have three bottles of vanilla. You bought the second because you couldn't find the first, and the third because you'd forgotten about the second.

Multiply that across a pantry, a cleaning closet, and two bathrooms and you have the actual cost of not knowing what you own.

---

## What it's really costing you

**Money you spend twice.** Every duplicate is a thing you already paid for. Nobody notices it happening because each one is small — a spice, a bottle of detergent, another pack of batteries.

**Food you throw out.** Things go bad at the back where nobody sees them. Not because anyone is careless — because there's no way to see what's there without taking everything out.

**The emergency trip.** You're out of something you needed today. That's an hour and a special trip, and it happens because nothing told you it was running low a week ago.

**The asking.** Where's the extra toilet paper. Do we have more coffee. Did anyone buy tinfoil. Every one of those questions comes to you, because the answers live in your head.

That last one is the real cost, and it's the one nobody counts. It's not a task. It's a background process that never switches off.

---

## And if something happens

If there's a fire, a flood, or a break-in, your insurance company will ask you to list what you lost.

Try doing that from memory, from a hotel room, three days after. Most people can't get past the obvious things and end up claiming a fraction of what they actually owned.

A record you made when nothing was wrong is worth a great deal at that moment. Keep a copy somewhere other than the house.

---

## Why paper doesn't hold

People do try. A notebook, a spreadsheet, a list on the fridge.

It fails for the same reason every time: **only one person can update it, and only when they're standing in front of it.** Your husband takes the last of something and doesn't write it down. The cleaner uses the last bottle of floor cleaner. Nobody's being difficult — the list just isn't where they are.

A digital record works because everyone has their phone in their hand at the moment the thing runs out.

---

## What it actually needs to do

**Show what's low, not just what you own.** A list of contents is a record. A list of what's running out is a shopping list.

**Let more than one person update it.** If it's on your phone only, it's your job forever.

**Take seconds per item.** More than that and nobody does it, including you.

**Hold the kashrus.** Meat, dairy and pareve on every item, visible at a glance, with locations that reflect a kitchen that actually has two of everything. An app that treats "the kitchen" as one place cannot describe your kitchen.

---

## Start small

One shelf. Not the house.

Consumables first — the things that run out. Set a low-stock alert on the ten things you never want to be without. Add everyone else who touches those shelves on day one, not after you've perfected it.

Leave it two weeks. If it's still accurate, add a shelf.

Most people fail at this by starting with the whole house and stopping at forty percent. Fifty items you maintain are worth more than four hundred you catalogued once.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why does a home need a digital inventory?

Because not knowing what you own costs money in duplicate purchases, food that spoils unseen, and emergency trips for things that ran out without warning. It also puts every "where is the…" question through one person, which is the largest cost and the one nobody counts.

### Isn't a spreadsheet or notebook enough?

It works until someone else uses the last of something. Paper lists can only be updated by one person standing in front of them, so they drift out of date immediately. A digital record works because everyone has their phone with them at the moment a thing runs out.

### What should I put in it first?

Consumables — food, cleaning supplies, paper goods, toiletries. Those are what run out, get double-bought, and go missing. Start with one shelf, not the whole house, and add more only once the first one has stayed accurate for a fortnight.

### Does a home inventory help with insurance?

Yes. After a fire, flood or theft your insurer asks you to list what you lost, and doing that from memory days later means most people claim far less than they owned. Make the record while nothing is wrong and keep a copy somewhere other than the house.
