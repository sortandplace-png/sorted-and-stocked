# Moving Into A New Home Checklist

Everyone plans the move. Almost nobody plans the house.

You've booked the truck and labelled the boxes. Then you're standing in an unfamiliar kitchen at nine at night and you don't know where the water shut-off is, whether the smoke alarms work, or who to call if something floods.

This is the checklist for the house, not the boxes.

---

## The first hour

**Find and photograph five things.** Water main shut-off. Electrical panel — and check whether the breakers are actually labelled, because they're usually wrong. Gas shut-off. HVAC unit and filter slot, noting the filter size. Water heater.

Photograph each one. "It's in the basement" is useless to a plumber on the phone.

**Test every smoke and carbon monoxide alarm**, and check the manufacture date on the back. Alarms have a service life of about ten years regardless of batteries, and previous owners rarely replace them.

**Change the locks.** You have no idea how many keys exist. This is the one non-negotiable on the list.

---

## Utilities

Electric, gas, water, trash, internet, security. **Book internet earliest** — it has the longest lead time of anything.

Give yourself a day of overlap at both ends. Paying an extra day of electricity is trivial; arriving at a dark, unheated house is not.

**Note the meter readings on day one.** It's your only defence against a disputed first bill.

---

## The systems you've inherited

You've taken over a machine somebody else was running.

**Photograph the model plate on every appliance** — brand, model, serial, rough age. It's always on a sticker behind something, and every repair call and part order starts with that number.

**Write down the consumables:** HVAC filter size, fridge filter model, any unusual bulb types. These are the things you'll be standing in a hardware store guessing about.

**Find out what's on a schedule.** When was the HVAC last serviced, the chimney last swept, the septic last inspected. If there's no documentation, assume nothing was done and start your own clock.

---

## Before you need them

**Nearest urgent care and pharmacy.** Actually look, don't assume.

**Build the vendor list now** — plumber, electrician, HVAC, handyman, appliance repair. Finding a plumber at eleven at night with water coming through a ceiling is not the moment to be reading reviews.

**Local practicalities:** bin days, parking rules, the school bus stop, whether packages are safe on the step.

---

## For a frum household

**Eruv boundaries** — confirm the house is inside and where the line runs.

**Shul walking distances**, not driving distances, and which minyanim run when.

**Schools and the bus routes** that serve this address.

**Kosher groceries and delivery ranges. Mikveh location and hours.**

**And the kitchen** — this is the one real chance to set separation up physically rather than working around a layout. Decide which cabinets, drawers, counters and sinks carry which designation before anything goes in.

Any question about the kitchen you've inherited, or what requires what, goes to your Rav before you use anything.

---

## The first month

Replace the HVAC filter and any alarm over eight years old. Check under every sink. Run every fixture and appliance once. Clean the dryer vent — assume it hasn't been. Walk the exterior looking for water.

**Then start your own maintenance calendar.** You've inherited a house whose service history you don't know. Starting a record now means that in three years you can answer questions the previous owner couldn't.

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should I do first when moving into a new home?

Before unpacking anything, find and photograph the water main shut-off, electrical panel, gas shut-off, HVAC filter slot and water heater. Then test every smoke and carbon monoxide alarm, check their manufacture dates, and change the locks.

### Should I change the locks on a new house?

Yes, without exception. You have no way of knowing how many keys exist or who holds them — previous owners, contractors, agents, cleaners. Change every exterior lock and reset garage, keypad and alarm codes.

### What should I record about the appliances?

Brand, model, serial number and rough age for every major appliance, plus HVAC filter size, fridge filter model and any unusual bulb types. Photograph each model plate in the first week — that number starts every repair call and part order.

### What should a frum family check about a new house?

Eruv boundaries and where the line runs, walking distance to shul rather than driving distance, schools and bus routes for the actual address, kosher grocery delivery ranges, and mikveh hours. The kitchen is also the one chance to design separation physically — decide designations before anything goes into a cabinet, and take any question of status to your Rav.
