# Decluttering Before Moving: What To Get Rid Of

You are about to pay someone, by weight and by hour, to carry your things to a new house.

That's the clearest financial case for decluttering you will ever have. Everything you don't take is money you don't spend twice — once to move it, once to store it somewhere you haven't seen yet.

---

## Start where you don't live

Garage, basement, storage, back of closets, guest room.

Nothing in those places is needed before the move, so you can work through them without disrupting the house. They're also where the least-loved things are, which makes them the easiest decisions and good practice for the harder rooms.

---

## What almost always goes

**Anything broken you meant to fix.** You've had months. The new house doesn't change that.

**Duplicates.** You're about to find out how many you have. Keep the good one.

**Furniture that doesn't fit the new place.** Measure first. Moving a sofa that won't go in is the most expensive mistake on this list.

**Anything you'd have to store.** If it goes straight into the new garage untouched, ask why it's going at all.

**Half-used consumables.** Cleaning supplies, pantry odds, toiletries. Use them up or leave them. Don't pay to move liquids.

**Paperwork you'll never open.** This is the heaviest category per cubic foot in most houses.

---

## The question that decides it

Not *do I want this*. **Would I pay to move this, and then find a place for it?**

That's a different question and it gets a different answer. Wanting something is free; moving it isn't.

---

## The hard categories

**Children's things.** Involve them, and give them a defined amount of space rather than a rule — "what fits in this box comes" works far better than negotiating item by item.

**Inherited things.** Take a photograph, keep one piece rather than the set, or pass it to someone in the family who'll actually use it. All three beat moving a box you'll never open again.

**Books.** Heavy, expensive to move, and the ones you'll genuinely reread are a fraction of the shelf.

---

## Get it out before the truck comes

Donate, sell, or bin — but on a deadline that's before moving day, not after.

Anything unsold three weeks out gets donated. Anything undecided goes in a labelled box that gets opened at the new house within a month — and if it doesn't get opened, it goes.

**Don't move the undecided pile twice.**

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should I get rid of before moving?

Anything broken you meant to fix, duplicates, furniture that won't fit the new place, half-used consumables, paperwork you'll never open, and anything that would go straight into storage untouched. Measure before deciding about large furniture — moving a sofa that doesn't fit is the most expensive mistake available.

### Where should I start decluttering before a move?

The rooms you don't live in — garage, basement, storage, back of closets. Nothing there is needed before the move so it doesn't disrupt the house, and the decisions are easier, which is good practice for the harder rooms.

### What's the best question to ask when deciding?

Not whether you want it, but whether you'd pay to move it and then find a place for it. Wanting something is free; moving it isn't, and the two questions get different answers.

### How do I handle children's things and inherited items?

Give children a defined amount of space rather than negotiating item by item — what fits in this box comes. For inherited things, photograph them, keep one piece rather than a set, or pass them to a family member who'll use them.
