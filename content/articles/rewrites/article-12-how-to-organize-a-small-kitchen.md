# How To Organize A Small Kitchen

A small kitchen isn't a big kitchen with less room. It's a different problem, and the usual advice makes it worse.

Every organizing product sold for kitchens assumes you have somewhere to put the product.

---

## The constraint changes the answer

In a large kitchen you can afford to keep things you rarely use. In a small one, every item you keep costs you access to something you use daily.

So the first question isn't where to put things. **It's what leaves.**

---

## What goes first

**Duplicates.** Two peelers, three spatulas, four sets of measuring spoons. Keep one good one.

**Single-purpose gadgets.** If a knife does the job, the gadget is renting space it hasn't earned.

**Anything used twice a year.** It doesn't have to leave the house — it has to leave the kitchen. A labelled bin in a hall closet costs nothing and frees prime space. People forget that's an option.

**Serving pieces for guests you have twice a year.** Same answer.

---

## Then use the space you're not using

**Vertical.** Most small kitchens waste the space between shelf and shelf. Risers, stacking, under-shelf baskets.

**Inside cupboard doors.** Hooks, thin racks, lids.

**The wall.** Rails for utensils, magnetic strips for knives. Anything hanging isn't taking counter or drawer.

**Above the cupboards.** The things you genuinely use rarely, in labelled bins, so height stops being wasted.

---

## Protect the counter

In a small kitchen the counter is the only place work happens.

**Nothing lives on it that isn't used daily.** Not the mixer, not the fruit bowl if it's crowding you, not the mail — never the mail.

Every appliance on the counter is a piece of workspace you don't have. If you use it weekly rather than daily, it belongs in a cupboard.

---

## For a small kosher kitchen

The separation still has to be real, and that's harder with less room.

**Vertical separation works when horizontal doesn't** — different shelves rather than different sides. Colour-coding does a lot of work in a tight space, and clear labelling does more.

Pesach and Yom Tov sets belong out of the kitchen entirely in sealed labelled bins. In a small kitchen that isn't a preference, it's how the room stays usable.

Anything on status or what requires what goes to your Rav.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I organize a small kitchen?

Start with what leaves rather than where things go. In a small kitchen every item you keep costs you access to something you use daily, so duplicates, single-purpose gadgets and anything used twice a year go first — out of the kitchen, not necessarily out of the house.

### What should I get rid of in a small kitchen?

Duplicates beyond one good version, gadgets a knife could replace, and anything used twice a year. Occasional items can move to a labelled bin in a hall closet, which costs nothing and frees your best space. People forget storing outside the kitchen is an option.

### How do I make more storage in a small kitchen?

Use the space you aren't using: vertical space between shelves with risers and under-shelf baskets, the inside of cupboard doors, wall rails and magnetic strips, and labelled bins above the cupboards for genuinely rare items.

### How do I keep separation in a small kosher kitchen?

Vertical separation where horizontal isn't possible — different shelves rather than different sides — with colour-coding and clear labelling doing more work than they would in a large kitchen. Pesach and Yom Tov sets belong out of the kitchen entirely. Questions of status go to your Rav.
