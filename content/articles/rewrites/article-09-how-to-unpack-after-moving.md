# How To Unpack After Moving Without Chaos

The boxes will get emptied either way. What decides the next two years is the order you do it in — and whether things go into a system or just out of a box.

Most people unpack by whichever box is nearest. Six months later they're still finding things in the wrong rooms.

---

## Night one: three things only

**Beds made. One bathroom working. Something to eat and drink.**

That's it. Everything else waits. Trying to do more on the first night is how people wake up exhausted in a house that still looks like a warehouse.

---

## Day one: kitchen

Not because it's urgent — because everything else is easier once you can feed people.

**And unpack it into a plan, not out of a box.** Decide where things go before you put them there: what's at eye level, which drawer is utensils, where the everyday dishes live.

**For a kosher kitchen this is the moment.** You will never again have this much freedom to design the separation physically rather than working around an existing layout. Decide the designations for every cabinet, drawer, counter and sink before anything goes in — and label as you place, not afterwards.

Anything about the status of the kitchen you've arrived at goes to your Rav.

---

## Then bedrooms

People function better after a proper night's sleep in a room that isn't full of boxes. Beds, clothes, a lamp.

Children's rooms early — a child with their own things unpacked settles into the whole house faster.

---

## Then everything else, slowly

Bathrooms, living areas, then the rooms nobody sees.

**The garage and storage go last**, and here's the useful part: whatever is still in a box after a month is a genuine answer to whether you needed it. Don't unpack it out of duty. Donate it.

---

## Rules that stop the chaos

**Empty boxes leave the house daily.** Flattened, out, gone. Boxes accumulating in a hallway make the whole house feel unfinished long after it is.

**Finish a room before starting another.** A house with four rooms at seventy percent feels far worse than two rooms done and two untouched.

**Don't put anything down "for now."** For now is how the next three years of clutter starts, on day four.

**Stop at a fixed time each day.** Unpacking has no natural end and will absorb every hour you let it.

---

## Set the routines while everything is still fluid

You get one window where nothing is habitual yet.

**Where paper lands. Where keys and bags go. The fifteen-minute evening reset.** Establish those in the first month and they hold for years. Retrofit them later and you're fighting a habit.

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should I unpack first after moving?

On the first night, only three things: beds made, one bathroom working, and something to eat. Then the kitchen on day one, because everything else is easier once you can feed people, then bedrooms, then everything else.

### How do I unpack without creating chaos?

Finish one room before starting another, take flattened boxes out of the house daily, never put anything down 'for now', and stop at a fixed time each day. Unpacking has no natural end and will absorb every hour you give it.

### What if boxes are still packed after a month?

That's a genuine answer about whether you needed the contents. Don't unpack them out of duty — donate them. The garage and storage should be last for exactly this reason.

### When should I set up household routines in a new home?

In the first month, while nothing is habitual yet. Where paper lands, where keys and bags go, and a fifteen-minute evening reset. Established then they hold for years; retrofitted later you're fighting an existing habit.
