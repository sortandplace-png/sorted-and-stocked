# How To Create A Pantry Inventory System

You don't need to know about everything in your house. You need to know about the things that run out.

That's the whole difference between an inventory system that survives and one that gets abandoned in week three.

---

## Track consumables only

Food, cleaning supplies, paper goods, toiletries. The things that get used up, re-bought unnecessarily, and discovered missing at the worst moment.

Not plates. Not the mixer. Those don't run out, so tracking them earns you nothing.

**Fifty items you keep accurate beats four hundred you catalogued once.**

---

## What each item needs

**What it is.** Specific enough to buy the right one — brand and size if it matters, not just "rice".

**Where it lives.** The actual shelf, not "the pantry".

**How much is low.** The number where you'd want to know. Two rolls of foil left, not zero.

That's three fields. Anything more and nobody fills it in.

---

## The part everyone skips

**Somebody has to update it when they take the last one.**

That's the whole system. If your husband finishes the coffee and nothing gets recorded, the inventory is wrong and you're back to checking shelves yourself.

Which means: everyone who takes things needs to be able to update it, from their own phone, in a few seconds. If it lives on your phone alone, it's your job forever and it will fail.

**Written in Spanish too if that's what your staff reads.**

---

## Build it in three passes

**One shelf.** Enter what's on it. Set low-stock numbers on the things you never want to be without.

**Leave it a fortnight.** Did it stay accurate? Did anyone else update it? That's the real test, and it's better to fail it on one shelf than on a whole house.

**Then add the next shelf.** And keep going only as long as it stays true.

---

## For a kosher kitchen

Every item needs its designation on it — meat, dairy or pareve — and locations that reflect a kitchen with two of everything.

Sets that only come out for Pesach or Yom Tov are worth tracking separately, because the question you'll ask about them is "do we have enough" once a year, not "are we low" weekly.

---

## Why it's worth it

Duplicate buying stops. Food stops spoiling unseen at the back. The emergency trip for something that ran out last week stops.

And the questions stop coming to you — which is the part nobody counts and the part that costs the most.

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should a pantry inventory system track?

Consumables only — food, cleaning supplies, paper goods, toiletries. Things that get used up, re-bought unnecessarily, and discovered missing at the worst moment. Not plates or appliances; those don't run out, so tracking them earns nothing.

### How much detail does each item need?

Three fields: what it is with enough specificity to buy the right one, where it actually lives down to the shelf, and the level at which you'd want to know it's low. Anything beyond that and nobody fills it in.

### Why do pantry inventory systems stop working?

Because nobody updates it when they take the last one. If it lives on one person's phone it stays that person's job and drifts out of date immediately. Everyone who takes things needs to be able to update it in seconds, from their own phone.

### How do I start without it taking over my life?

One shelf. Enter it, set low-stock levels, then leave it a fortnight and see whether it stayed accurate and whether anyone else updated it. Better to fail that test on one shelf than on a whole house. Add the next shelf only if it passed.
