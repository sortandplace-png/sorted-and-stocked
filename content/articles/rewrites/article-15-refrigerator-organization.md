# Refrigerator Organization: The Complete Guide

A fridge gets disorganized faster than anything else in the kitchen, because things go in wet, cold, in a hurry, and usually while holding something else.

So the system has to survive being used badly.

---

## Cold isn't even

The door is the warmest part of the fridge. It swings into a warm room several times a day.

**So the door is for things that tolerate it** — condiments, jams, drinks. Not milk, and not eggs, whatever the moulded egg tray suggests.

**The back is the coldest.** Things you want kept properly cold go there.

**The bottom shelf is coldest of the main shelves** — and it's also where raw meat belongs, so nothing can drip onto anything below it.

---

## Give things a place

**Eye level: leftovers and things to eat soon.** If you can't see it, you won't eat it, and that's most of what gets thrown away.

**One "eat me first" bin.** Anything near its end goes in there. It's the single change that cuts waste most, because the alternative is discovering things too late at the back.

**Drawers do what they say.** Crisper drawers control humidity — high for leafy things, low for fruit. Most people never touch the slider.

**Dairy together, meat together**, kept properly separate, each in its own defined zone rather than wherever there's room.

---

## Temperature

Fridge at or below 40°F, freezer at 0°F. Bacteria multiply fastest between 40°F and 140°F, which is why the door being warmer matters.

**Buy a fridge thermometer.** The dial on the inside wall isn't a temperature — it's a setting, and it's often wrong.

---

## Keeping it true

**Ten minutes before the shop.** Not after. You need to know what's in there before you buy more, and it's when things near their end are easiest to spot.

**Wipe as you go**, while shelves are empty during that ten minutes.

**Clear containers.** You don't open what you can't see into, and unopened is how leftovers become waste.

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How should I organize my refrigerator?

Work with where the cold actually is: the door is warmest so it takes condiments and drinks rather than milk or eggs, the back and bottom shelf are coldest, and raw meat belongs on the bottom so nothing drips below it. Keep leftovers at eye level, because what you can't see is what gets thrown away.

### What temperature should my fridge and freezer be?

At or below 40°F for the fridge and 0°F for the freezer. Bacteria multiply fastest between 40°F and 140°F. Buy a fridge thermometer — the dial inside is a setting, not a temperature, and it's often wrong.

### What's the easiest way to stop wasting food in the fridge?

One 'eat me first' bin at eye level. Anything near its end goes in there, so it gets used rather than discovered too late at the back. Clear containers help for the same reason — nobody opens what they can't see into.

### When should I clean out the fridge?

Ten minutes before the shop, not after. You need to know what's there before you buy more, and it's the moment when things near their end are easiest to spot. Wipe the shelves while they're empty.
