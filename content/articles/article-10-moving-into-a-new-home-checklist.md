# Moving Into A New Home Checklist

Everyone plans the move. Almost nobody plans the house.

You've booked the truck, labelled the boxes, and worked out which room everything goes in. Then you're standing in an unfamiliar kitchen at 9 p.m. on day one and you don't know where the water shut-off is, whether the smoke detectors work, what size filter the HVAC takes, or who to call if something floods.

**This new home checklist is about the house itself, not the boxes.** Utilities and accounts, safety and security, the systems you inherit, the local services you'll need, and the first month of things that only become urgent once you've missed them.

Unpacking is a separate job with its own sequence — that's covered in **[how to unpack after moving](/unpacking-after-moving)**. This is everything else.

## Before You Move In

Ideally done at a walkthrough, or in the first hours before the truck arrives.

### Find these five things and photograph them

- **Water main shut-off** — the single most important one. If a pipe fails at 2 a.m., knowing this saves a floor.
- **Electrical panel** — and check whether the breakers are actually labelled. Usually they're wrong.
- **Gas shut-off**, if applicable
- **HVAC unit and filter slot** — note the filter size while you're there
- **Water heater** — note the model and the age if it's visible

**Photograph each one.** "It's in the basement" is useless to a plumber on the phone or to a spouse who wasn't there.

### Test the safety equipment

- Press the test button on every smoke and carbon monoxide alarm
- Check the manufacture date on each — alarms have a [service life of about ten years](https://www.nfpa.org/education-and-research/home-fire-safety/smoke-alarms) regardless of battery changes, and previous owners rarely replace them
- Locate fire extinguishers, or buy one for the kitchen
- Check that every window and exterior door locks properly

### Security

- **Change the locks.** You have no idea how many keys exist. This is the one non-negotiable on the list.
- Change or reprogram garage codes and any keypad entries
- Reset the alarm system codes and get it registered in your name
- Note any lockbox that's still attached and get it removed

## Utilities and Accounts

Most of this should be arranged before moving day, but here's the full list to confirm against.

**Set up and confirm active:**
- Electricity
- Gas
- Water and sewer
- Trash and recycling — and find out which day, and where the bins go
- Internet — **book this earliest; it routinely has the longest lead time of anything**
- Home security monitoring, if applicable

**Give yourself a one-day overlap at both ends.** Paying an extra day of electricity is trivial; arriving at a dark, unheated house is not.

**Note the meter readings on day one** for electric, gas, and water. It's the only defence against a disputed first bill.

### Change of address

- Postal service forwarding
- Bank, credit cards, investments
- Insurance — home, auto, health
- Employer and payroll
- Driver's licence and vehicle registration — most states have a deadline, often 30 days
- Voter registration
- Pharmacy
- Subscriptions and deliveries
- Anything that sends you something annually, which is the category people forget

## The Systems You've Inherited

You've just taken over a machine somebody else was running. Find out what you've got.

### Build the appliance record

For every major appliance and system: **brand, model number, serial number, approximate age, and warranty status if known.**

**Photograph the model plate on each one the week you move in.** It's always on a sticker you have to crawl behind something to read, and every repair call, part order, and warranty claim starts with that number.

Covers: HVAC, water heater, washer, dryer, refrigerator, dishwasher, oven, garbage disposal, sump pump, water softener, generator.

### Note the consumables

- HVAC filter size and type — write it somewhere you'll find it
- Refrigerator water filter model
- Any whole-house water filter
- Light bulb types for anything unusual — recessed, dimmable, specialty fixtures

**These are small and they're the things you'll be standing in a hardware store guessing about at some point.**

### Find out what's on a schedule

- When was the HVAC last serviced?
- When was the chimney last swept, if there is one? [Annual inspection is the standard recommendation](https://www.csia.org/homeowner-resources/)
- Is there a septic system, and when was it last inspected?
- Are there any service contracts you're inheriting?

If the previous owners left documentation, keep it. If they didn't, assume nothing has been done and start your own clock.

## First Week: The Local Layer

None of this is urgent until it is, and then it's very urgent.

### Find and save

- **Nearest urgent care and emergency room** — actually look, don't assume
- **Pharmacy**, and transfer your prescriptions
- **Grocery store** you'll actually use, plus the kosher grocery or the nearest source for what you need
- **Pediatrician and family doctor**, if you've moved out of range
- **Dentist**
- **Vet**, if applicable

### Build the vendor list before you need it

- Plumber
- Electrician
- HVAC
- Handyman
- Appliance repair
- Lawn or snow removal

**Finding a plumber at 11 p.m. with water coming through a ceiling is not the moment to be reading reviews.** Ask neighbours, ask whoever sold you the house, ask a local group. Twenty minutes now.

### Practical local knowledge

- Trash and recycling days, and where bins go
- Street parking rules, permits, snow bans
- School bus stop and times
- Any HOA or community rules you're now subject to
- Where the mail actually gets delivered, and whether packages are safe

## For Frum Households

A few things worth sorting early, because they're harder to fix later.

- **Eruv boundaries** — confirm whether the house is inside, and where the line runs
- **Shul options and distances** — walking distances specifically, and which minyanim run when
- **Schools**, and the bus routes that serve the address
- **Kosher groceries and takeout**, and delivery ranges
- **Mikveh location and hours**
- **Neighbours** — the practical value of knowing who's nearby before a Yom Tov or an emergency is hard to overstate

**And the kitchen:** a new kitchen is the one real opportunity to set up separation physically rather than working around an existing layout. Decide before anything goes into a cabinet which cabinets, drawers, counters, and sinks carry which designation, and label as you place rather than afterwards.

**Any question about the status of the kitchen you've inherited, kashering, or what's required goes to your Rav.** Ask before you use anything you're unsure about, not after.

## First Month: The Things That Become Urgent

Set these as reminders now.

**Week two:**
- Replace HVAC filter — assume the one in there is old
- Replace any smoke alarm older than eight years
- Deep clean anything the previous occupants didn't
- Register appliances for warranty where possible

**Week three:**
- Locate and test the sump pump, if there is one
- Check under every sink for existing leaks or old damage
- Run every faucet, flush every toilet, run the dishwasher and washer empty once
- Check the dryer vent — assume it hasn't been cleaned

**Week four:**
- Walk the exterior: roof from the ground, gutters, grading, any obvious water issues
- Confirm insurance is active and the coverage matches what you actually own
- Start the maintenance calendar — monthly, quarterly, semi-annual, annual

**The maintenance calendar is the one that pays.** You've just inherited a house whose service history you don't know. Starting your own record from day one means that in three years you'll be able to answer questions the previous owner couldn't.

## Set Up the Household Systems Now

This is the part that separates a house you moved into from a home that works — and the window closes fast.

You're setting up every routine from scratch, with no accumulated habits and no "we've always done it that way." That's an opportunity you get maybe twice in a decade.

**In the first month, establish:**

- **Where the paper lands** — mail, forms, school papers, before it reaches a counter
- **A landing zone** for keys, bags, and shoes
- **The evening reset** — fifteen minutes, and it's easier to start now than to retrofit
- **The weekly planning session** — calendar, meals, list
- **A household binder** — the emergency information, vendor contacts, and appliance records you've just gathered. You're already holding most of it; write it down while it's in front of you.

That last one is genuinely easiest right now. You've just photographed the shut-offs, recorded the model numbers, and built a vendor list. **That's most of a household binder already — spend an hour and it exists.**

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should I do first when moving into a new home?

Before unpacking anything, locate and photograph the water main shut-off, electrical panel, gas shut-off, HVAC filter slot, and water heater. Then test every smoke and carbon monoxide alarm and change the locks. These take under an hour and they're the things you'll need urgently at the worst possible moment if you skip them.

### Should I change the locks on a new house?

Yes, without exception. You have no way of knowing how many keys exist or who holds them — previous owners, contractors, agents, cleaners, neighbours. Change or rekey every exterior lock, reprogram garage and keypad codes, and reset any alarm codes to your own.

### What utilities do I need to set up when moving?

Electricity, gas, water and sewer, trash and recycling, internet, and security monitoring if applicable. Book internet earliest — it routinely has the longest lead time. Arrange a one-day overlap at both the old and new address, and record meter readings on day one to protect against a disputed first bill.

### What should I record about my new home's appliances?

Brand, model number, serial number, approximate age, and warranty status for every major appliance and system, plus HVAC filter size, refrigerator water filter model, and any unusual light bulb types. Photograph the model plate on each appliance during the first week — that number starts every repair call, part order, and warranty claim.

### How soon do I need to update my address and licence?

File postal forwarding before you move, and update your driver's licence and vehicle registration promptly — most states set a deadline, often around 30 days. Then work through banks, insurance, employer, pharmacy, subscriptions, and anything that contacts you only once a year, which is the category most people forget.

### What maintenance should I do in the first month of a new house?

Replace the HVAC filter and any smoke alarm older than eight years, check under every sink for leaks, run every fixture and appliance once, clean the dryer vent, and walk the exterior for water issues. Then start your own maintenance calendar — you've inherited a house whose service history you don't know.

### What's the best time to set up household routines in a new home?

The first month, because you're establishing everything from scratch with no accumulated habits to work around. Set where paper lands, a landing zone for keys and bags, an evening reset, and a weekly planning session. It's far easier to start these now than to retrofit them a year in.
